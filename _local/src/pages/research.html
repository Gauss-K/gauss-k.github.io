The programme

Complex Relativity (CR) is a proposed framework for exploring complex spacetime and its possible connections to light, matter, gravitation, and the foundations of physics.

It begins with quantities an observer can measure. Light traces out a causal cone. A free particle has a real momentum and a real mass. The question is how much these facts tell us about the underlying geometry.

The two postulates

The starting assumptions fix what the real and complex descriptions must share.

P1

A shared light cone

Physical observers use a real Lorentzian geometry. Its light cone is also the light cone of a complex metric.

P2

Real momentum and mass

Physical momenta and masses remain real. Their mass-shell constraints must be compatible with the complex formulation.
